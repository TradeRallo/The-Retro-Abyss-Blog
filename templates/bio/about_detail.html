About Saigotsuki Reiga

Prologue

Reiga Saigotsuki was born on December 14, 1988, in the Sumiyoshi Ward of Osaka, into a well-off family. He was the couple's first child. His father, Takashi Saigotsuki (born 1965), worked as an engineer at Yamaha, while his mother, Ayaka Saigotsuki (born April 5, 1967), was a designer, also at Yamaha.

His father was passionate about musical instruments, particularly synthesizers; he even kept one in his home study and loved playing it. He also had a keen interest in electronics—enjoying soldering circuit boards and even assembling his own computers—and dabbled in auto mechanics. In his spare time, he loved playing arcade games (later moving on to shooters and RTS titles) on the Nintendo Famicom.

His mother had loved drawing since childhood, especially cartoons, and shifted to creating anime-style art during her teenage years. At work, she frequently designed advertising banners, posters, and logos. After Reiga was born and she returned from maternity leave, she transitioned to digital art. She was initially self-taught but later attended art school as a teenager. She shared her husband's love for video games; like him, she played on consoles, with a particular fondness for fantasy games.

From early childhood, Reiga observed his parents' hobbies. He was a quiet yet curious child. His toys included construction vehicles, a simple building-block set similar to Lego, and a Game Boy purchased in 1991. At first, he simply watched his parents play, but later he began trying it out for himself.

The first thing that truly captivated Reiga was watching his father play the synthesizer. His father noticed his interest as early as age three and would sometimes let him "play around" on the keyboard. Around that same time, Reiga began to take an interest in anime, manga, and video games. His first game was Tetris, as it was easy to pick up. Two years later, he tried to beat Super Mario Land, though he still needed his father's help to do so.

He first discovered anime while watching his mother view Sailor Moon. Sometimes he would sit beside her and watch, gazing at the colorful characters. His father didn't mind anime—and would occasionally watch it with them—though he generally preferred to focus on his own hobbies. However, he was curious about how the music for such shows was created, and he would sometimes show Reiga how synthesizers worked and how animation was utilized.

Even before Reiga was born, his father owned a Sharp X68K home computer, as well as another PC—which he had built himself—for tasks specific to his work.

From the age of three, Reiga attended yochien (kindergarten) until he was old enough for elementary school. He spent much of his time playing with building sets and experimenting with various musical instruments. Kindergarten also taught him how to work effectively within a group.

At age five, Reiga began learning to play the synthesizer under his father's guidance. After just a few months of lessons, he was able to play simple melodies on his own.

Before long, Reiga started watching cartoons on TV by himself. His early favorites included Dragon Ball and Tom and Jerry, though he would also frequently sneak a peek at Sailor Moon.

At five, Reiga was entrusted with a Game Boy. Although he could have played for hours on end, he rarely played for more than two hours at a time; he would eventually grow tired of the game and switch to a different activity. Occasionally, his father would borrow the handheld console. Furthermore, whenever a game proved too difficult or he got stuck on a tough level, Reiga would simply put it aside and move on to something else.

Reiga started school at the age of six (in 1995), marking the beginning of a new chapter in his life. Schoolwork presented a new challenge, but he adapted quickly. While he was initially quiet and somewhat reserved, he eventually began to bond with his classmates. Whereas he might have previously spent his after-school hours gaming or playing music, he now devoted more time to his studies.

On the whole, Reiga was a good student; he particularly loved subjects such as his native language, shop class, mathematics, and home economics. He also did reasonably well in his other subjects, though he didn't devote as much attention to them.

In 1996, Reiga’s father upgraded the family computer by replacing its components and installing Windows 95. Before that, the PC had been used solely by his father, as the previous operating system was difficult to navigate.

Around the same time, Reiga’s mother bought a graphics tablet and began learning digital art in Photoshop, having previously focused on traditional drawing at home.

Watching her create anime-style illustrations gradually sparked Reiga’s interest in drawing.

At the same time, he watched his father play various PC games—such as Doom, Final Fantasy, Quake, and Command & Conquer. Back in 1995, his father had bought a PlayStation, putting away the old Nintendo Famicom, and played exclusive titles available only on the new console, such as Resident Evil.

Reiga began to take a serious interest in video games, and his father would let him play from time to time. His full-fledged passion for gaming began in 2001, when he received a PC built by his father for his thirteenth birthday.

Details regarding specific titles will be presented later in the timeline.

It is also worth noting the evolution of Reiga’s musical tastes. As a child, Reiga would listen to the melodies his father played on the synthesizer. Sometimes, his father would play songs by both Japanese and Western artists on his tape deck—either as background music while soldering circuit boards or as a reference for trying to play the tracks himself. His father loved synth-pop and rock; among his favorite bands were Van Halen, Dire Straits, X Japan, and TM Network. He also adored Michael Jackson, and cassettes of his music filled the shelves in his study. To be fair, Reiga’s mother shared his father’s tastes, though she leaned more toward pop music. Reiga listened to these songs with interest, laying the groundwork for his own future musical preferences.

By his early school years, Reiga was already able to play simple melodies on the piano during music class; teachers praised him, and his classmates admired his talent. Although Reiga was initially flustered by the spotlight, he soon found the attention encouraging and grew increasingly passionate about music. He played various tunes that appealed to him, often choosing pieces that blended classical and pop elements. However, Reiga wasn't the only musically gifted student at the school; there were others among his classmates and the student body who could sing or play instruments. This served as healthy motivation for Reiga to keep honing his skills and, perhaps, eventually create his own music.

One classmate worth mentioning was a girl who played both acoustic guitar and piano. Her name was Akira Suzuki, and her musical talent never failed to impress the other students. She frequently took part in school concerts and would gather small groups to perform popular songs. Although Reiga admired her talent, he also felt a sense of healthy competition, which motivated him to work hard on honing his own skills. Akira was always open and sociable; one day, while they were in the second grade, she suggested they play a duet—a proposal that marked a significant step forward for Reiga in the world of music.

From that point on, Reiga and Akira played various popular songs, growing and refining their abilities throughout their elementary school years. They often visited each other's homes, with the full approval of both sets of parents. During elementary school, they performed at concerts several times—sometimes together, sometimes separately. There was one unsuccessful performance where, while attempting a complex melody, nervousness caused them to lose synchronization. Nevertheless, their efforts were praised and recognized; they ultimately placed somewhere between fifth and tenth. This happened in the fourth grade.

Reiga developed a serious interest in anime at the age of twelve, in 2000, when he discovered more mature and profound genres aimed at a male audience. Unlike his earlier years, when he had only occasionally watched anime with his parents and friends, he now began selecting titles that truly captivated him. Some of the first anime to leave a lasting impression on him were Neon Genesis Evangelion, Cowboy Bebop, Gunsmith Cats, and Space Battleship Yamato. These series represented a crucial milestone, opening up new horizons in his appreciation of Japanese animation. Their complex plots, philosophical themes, and profound characters captivated Reiga, and it was then that he began to realize anime could be more than just entertainment—it could be a source of inspiration for his own creative work. His interest in anime began to grow when he started playing Final Fantasy.

In terms of feature-length animated and live-action films, Reiga watched Hayao Miyazaki’s movies (such as My Neighbor Totoro and Kiki's Delivery Service), Hedgehog in the Fog, Aladdin, and The Lion King on VHS. During early childhood, he watched The Terminator and Jurassic Park with his parents. In his early school years, he saw Mulan, Tarzan, The Matrix, and the first two Toy Story films in theaters, as well as Titanic with his parents. He was also a fan of the Star Wars universe, watching the feature films.

Reiga also experienced some memorable, positive events. In 1998, he tried using a PC for the first time; it had recently been upgraded and was now available for the whole family to use. The first game he completed was Doom, followed by Quake and Quake 2. A year earlier, he had started playing on the PlayStation, primarily completing Japanese titles such as Final Fantasy, Resident Evil, and Metal Gear Solid. Later, he became interested in other titles like Half-Life, Command & Conquer, and Gran Turismo.

In 1999, Reiga visited a technology exhibition in Tokyo with his class, where cutting-edge electronics and innovative developments were on display. He was particularly intrigued by the demonstrations of new computers, game consoles, and interactive technologies. This experience inspired him to take a serious interest in the world of technology and gadgets. Feeling that technology was his calling, he began studying electronics and programming more earnestly. Even while still in school, he started learning how devices worked—specifically PC hardware—and took his first steps in programming. That same year, he wrote his first game: a top-down 2D platformer in which the player had to escape from a maze. (He later named the project Reiga Maze 1999.)

Reiga first discovered electronic music in 2000 when, while walking past a nightclub, he heard the powerful rhythms of trance. That moment marked his entry into the world of EDM. He began seeking out similar music, immersing himself in the atmosphere of synthesized sounds, and soon headed to a store to buy trance CDs.

Noticing his interest, a sales clerk recommended The Chemical Brothers. Reiga bought one of their albums out of curiosity, and as soon as he played the first few tracks, he knew instantly that this was exactly what he had been looking for. Their dynamic, innovative compositions resonated with him immediately, further fueling his interest in the electronic music scene and inspiring him to explore the genre more deeply.

Around the year 2000, his parents bought mobile phones for themselves—and later for Reiga as well. Before that, the family had relied on a landline. Reiga’s first mobile phone was a Sharp J-SH04, which he received as a Christmas gift.

It is also worth noting that as Reiga’s musical tastes evolved, he was given a Sony Network Walkman (NM-1) MP3 player alongside a PC in 2001. Speaking of that PC—which his father assembled—it consisted of the following components: an Intel Pentium III processor, 256 MB of SDRAM, a 20 GB HDD, an Nvidia GeForce2 MX graphics card (32 MB VRAM), a Socket 370 motherboard with AGP 4x support, a 52x CD-ROM drive, a Creative Sound Blaster Live! sound card, a 17-inch CRT monitor (1024x768 resolution), a PS/2 keyboard, and a wired optical Logitech mouse.

Reiga had a small but tight-knit circle of friends. They played video games together and discussed technology, music, and anime. Sometimes they would gather at someone’s house to beat a new game on the PlayStation or PC, or to swap discs and magazines. Outdoors, they might ride bikes, hang out at arcades, or simply walk around the city discussing the latest trends in electronics and their favorite topics. Although Reiga was somewhat introverted, he valued his friends and made an effort to stay in touch with them, especially those who shared his interests.

Starting in early 2002, Reiga began to delve deeper into his preferred genres: trance and EDM. He wanted to start composing his own tracks, but there was a problem: obtaining a licensed copy of a DAW (Digital Audio Workstation) was no easy task. However, once his parents learned about his passion, they bought him a copy of Cubase SX. Following the manual and using a trial-and-error approach, Reiga gradually mastered the software and the fundamentals of mixing and mastering, eventually creating his first demos. He realized that he also needed to learn how to draw to create cover art and visuals for his tracks, so his mother began helping him with this; she also installed Photoshop on his PC and bought him a graphics tablet. Reiga managed to find information on forums regarding track mixing, which he applied to his first attempt at creating a full-fledged release.

Early Years (Aero Gen) – Retro Abyss

2003

After spending a long time studying the fundamentals of mixing and mastering, mastering VST plugins, and creating numerous demos, Reiga decided to produce his first full-fledged release. To this end, he launched a project named Retro Abyss. The word "Retro" evokes the past and nostalgia, while "Abyss" suggests something unexplored, profound, and perhaps even frightening. This contrast served as the project's core concept: music inspired by the past yet sounding as if it originated in another dimension or the future.

Work on the release began in October; the track took four weeks to compose, and creating the cover art took roughly the same amount of time. Titled "While Sakura is Blooming," the track was a trance composition. It was released on December 14, 2003. However, given the limited state of streaming platforms at the time and Reiga’s lack of distribution experience, he distributed his releases on burned CDs. Operating on a tight budget, Reiga handled the packaging himself—printing simple covers on photo paper or standard glossy paper and manually inserting them into plastic cases. Each copy was unique; Reiga strove for the highest possible quality and visual appeal despite his limited resources. Production runs were small—numbering only a few dozen copies—which he attempted to sell through acquaintances, online stores, and social gatherings, hoping his music would resonate with listeners. The inspiration for the release struck while he was walking through a park, listening to famous trance artists on his headphones.

The project also established a tradition of releasing at least one piece of artwork annually, exploring themes ranging from Reiga’s inner emotional experiences to various aesthetic interests. That same year, Reiga created artwork for the track "While Sakura is Blooming." At the time, such artwork had limited distribution, often appearing as small posters included with CD copies of releases.

Meanwhile, during this period, Reiga managed to do the following:

1. Try out new titles such as Command & Conquer: Generals (along with the Zero Hour expansion), SimCity 4, Need for Speed: Underground, Final Fantasy X-2, Deus Ex: Invisible War, GTA III, and Vice City. There were plenty of other titles Reiga completed before 2003, but they won't be listed here for now. Reiga also tried his hand at multiplayer games like CS 1.6, but he quickly realized they weren't really for him; quite often, his death count in matches exceeded his kill count. He experimented with various maps and weapons but ultimately felt that fast-paced shooters didn't suit his style. He was particularly impressed by GTA Vice City; its 80s aesthetic and music captivated him and would go on to partially influence his project, Retro Abyss.

2. By this time, Reiga was actively discovering artists like Paul van Dyk and Armin van Buuren through their releases. He also discovered bands such as Linkin Park, Nightwish, Korn, and—quite by accident—The Knife. As for Japanese artists, he had by then discovered Yellow Magic Orchestra, X Japan, and Ken Ishii.

3. That year, Reiga went to the cinema to see the animated film Finding Nemo with a school friend named Akira. Speaking of Akira—she was quietly falling for Reiga. However, the signals she sent were too subtle for him to pick up on, and he remained unaware of her feelings. He was simply too absorbed in his own project.

4. A year ago, when Reiga was just starting to try his hand at composing music, he upgraded his PC from Windows 98 SE to Windows XP.

5. Reiga discovered new anime titles, including Fullmetal Alchemist.

6. Reiga turned 15 this year. He hasn't had a serious relationship or his first kiss yet, though Akira, his childhood friend, has been flirting with him.

7. Also this year, Reiga's sister, Saigotsuki Hana, was born.

Q1 2004

After Christmas, Reiga resumed work on his own project. He began exploring other genres, including those found in releases by The Chemical Brothers. Although Reiga lacked inspiration for a time, he mastered new techniques for his future tracks—such as sound sampling, including samples from video games. "Aero" and, to some extent, "Afterburner" were among the tracks that utilized this method.

Meanwhile, Reiga continued selling the "While Sakura is Blooming" release on CD; notably, sales were quite good.

During this period, the following events took place in Reiga's life:

1. Reiga learned about the existence of 4chan from his classmates. He began using the platform around this time, having had fiber-optic internet access at home since 2001. He primarily visited the imageboard to connect with like-minded people and seek inspiration. He also discovered OCRemix, a platform where video game soundtrack remixes were posted.

2. Through 4chan, Reiga also discovered the Touhou universe. It intrigued him deeply; in fact, at least one of the three games he played that quarter was a title from this series.

3. In addition to Touhou 6: The Embodiment of Scarlet Devil, he played through The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind and Mafia: The City of Lost Heaven.

Q2 2004

Following the March release of Far Cry, Reiga bought the game on disc. Although it was in English, he managed to figure it out and successfully complete it. He was able to draw inspiration from the game for future releases. The first release idea that came to him was Dream Island; he planned to style this release like a soundtrack. Work on the soundtrack began in May. At the same time, ideas for the Aero and Afterburner releases were being developed. Concepts drawn from video games—specifically those with military themes—were used for these releases. In creating them, he utilized material from demo recordings dating back to 2002.

During this period, the following events took place in Reiga's life:

1. Reiga completed the aforementioned Far Cry as well as Tom Clancy's Splinter Cell.

2. He watched two anime titles.

3. During the holidays, Reiga decided to take a part-time job at a konbini (convenience store) to save up for a new phone.

Q3 2004

During this timeframe, the Aero and Dream Island releases were created—on July 7 and September 20, respectively. As digital distribution platforms did not yet exist, these releases were distributed in the same manner as While Sakura is Blooming; however, this time Reiga struck a deal with a local music rental shop, paying a distribution fee and offering a share of royalties for each disc sold. Meanwhile, he also designed the cover art and full-sized artwork for these releases. Additionally, Reiga created fan art of Reimu Hakurei, paying tribute to the Touhou universe, which he had become interested in.

During this period, the following events took place in Reiga's life:

1. Reiga played Doom 3, but found the game quite difficult to complete, leading to gaming burnout.

2. He watched the anime Bleach.

3. He vacationed in Okinawa with his parents.

Q4 2004

Starting in mid-October, Reiga began work on the mini-album Chronicles of the Rising Sun. Reiga decided not to overload the release with too many tracks, opting instead to include the previously created While Sakura is Blooming and Afterburner. This left just two more tracks to produce: Fuji and DUBlicated. He chose to style Fuji similarly to Afterburner, but decided to experiment with DUBlicated by adopting a techno style.

During this period, several things happened in Reiga’s life:

1. Reiga began to explore the work of Underworld and Ken Ishii in greater depth. This inspired his experimentation with DUBlicated. He started to realize that music could be not only "melodic" (like trance) but also "hypnotic" and "mechanical."

2. As the year drew to a close, his academic workload increased. Although Reiga had less free time, this actually helped him focus on Chronicles of the Rising Sun; the music became an outlet—a way to escape the pressures of school.

3. He remained in touch with a childhood friend, though a subtle, unspoken romantic tension lingered between them. She gave him a handmade birthday card, which he interpreted merely as a friendly gesture. In reality, it was a hint, but Reiga failed to pick up on it at the time.

4. During this period, Reiga watched the anime Elfen Lied but did not complete a single video game title.

Q1 2005

After the holidays, Reiga returned to Fuji and DUBlicated to add the finishing touches: tweaking the mix, selecting additional samples, and fine-tuning the mastering. He experimented with various effects for DUBlicated, drawing inspiration from the work of Underworld and Ken Ishii. At the same time, he began sketching out the cover art for the release. Reiga also started keeping an "idea journal" to jot down concepts for future releases and artwork.

During this period, several notable things happened in Reiga's life:

He played through Half-Life 2 and Need for Speed: Underground 2, and started playing GTA: San Andreas.

He became captivated by the anime Neon Genesis Evangelion, prompting him to note down an idea in his journal to remix the show's opening theme.

He visited Sapporo during the New Year holidays. The freezing temperatures, snow, and winter scenery inspired him to create "colder," more atmospheric sound textures, while walks through the city streets helped him shape the visual concepts for his future artwork.

Q2 2005

By May, the release—complete with cover art—was ready for distribution. Reiga burned the EP onto CDs, printed the covers, and packaged each copy by hand. He then sent some of the discs to a local music rental shop and distributed others to friends and acquaintances, building a "live audience" for Retro Abyss.

Two weeks after sales began, Reiga received an email from a local nightclub DJ inviting him to participate in a DJ set featuring his music.

Reiga agreed, feeling a mix of excitement and nerves, as this would be his first public performance. In early June, Reiga took the stage alongside a DJ.

The DJ showed him the basics of live track mixing; Reiga quickly got the hang of it and experienced, for the first time, the feeling of controlling sound in real time.

This event significantly boosted his confidence: he now saw that Retro Abyss could be more than just a studio project—it could be a live experience for listeners.

Inspired by this performance, Reiga created a signature piece of artwork for the year, dedicated to his first DJ set. The art captured the atmosphere of club lighting, high energy, and the crowd's excitement.

Q3 2005

Following the success of the Chronicles of the Rising Sun EP, Reiga felt that limiting himself to a single range of genres was dull, so he decided to be bolder in his experimentation. Listening to artists like Underworld, Ken Ishii, The Prodigy, and Nine Inch Nails showed him that music could span a vast emotional spectrum—ranging from aggressive and dark to hypnotic and experimental. These discoveries inspired Reiga to expand the Retro Abyss palette; the project needed to encompass various genres, not just trance.

He began to realize that Retro Abyss was not merely music, but an entire artistic world. For the project to evolve, it required a new philosophy, new visual identities, and genre experimentation. The time had come for a reformation and rebranding—a process that needed to be approached systematically.

The new direction for Retro Abyss began with the release of "Evangelion." Stylistically, the track remained close to the project's classic sound, yet it marked a shift in genre and experimentation. The release was ready in August 2005. By this time, the music distributor had officially partnered with iTunes, and Reiga’s tracks appeared on the platform for the first time, opening the project up to a wider audience.
